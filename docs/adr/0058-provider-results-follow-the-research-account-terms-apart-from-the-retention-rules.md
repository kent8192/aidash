# Provider results follow the Research account terms, apart from the Retention Rules

Decided on 2026-10-17 for #289. The Research account terms of a paid or declared Research provider state how long the service's results may be stored (`retention_days`) and how soon after the agreement ends they must be deleted (`deletion_after_termination_days`). They govern Provider results: what that service returned for one research attempt, as Aidash stores it. For Web search that is the attempt's recorded result and the invocation's stored tool result (titles, snippets, ranks, page ages and the list of addresses) and the titles of the Source records created from them. For Page reading through a declared Research provider it is the Document snapshot's contents and the text of its Evidence fragments. Pages Aidash's own page reader fetched by address are not Provider results. The account terms are the service's conditions, a basis separate from Aidash's Retention Rules (ADR 0045). Where both apply, whichever ends first decides.

Each attempt records the account and the `retention_days` in force when it was retrieved. A Provider result keeps its basis until the earlier of its retrieval plus the shorter of that recorded period and the current terms of the same account, if the account is still declared, and the account's termination plus `deletion_after_termination_days`. Shorter terms therefore apply to what is already kept, and longer terms never extend it. A removed declaration still expires its data by the recorded period. The termination date is a field of the account terms in the server settings, like the other agreement dates outside the version digest (ADR 0055). Aidash stores it the first time it reads it, so removing the declaration later does not undo it. From that date the account admits no further requests.

The sweeper on the existing retention loop replaces Provider results without a basis with tombstones and marks each record with the time and the reason `account_terms`. It keeps Source identities and addresses, attempt identities and charges, what Aidash itself sent, snapshot identities, retrieval times and digests, and each Evidence fragment's identity, line range and a digest of its text, recorded when the fragment is created. Citations can then still be verified from identities and digests and are shown as expired under the account terms. A record keeps the reason and time of its first purge, but an Evidence Revocation always replaces the reason with `revocation`. Unlike a revocation, the Run may keep researching. It may read a Source again as a new, approved and charged attempt, which reuses the Source record. A Purged snapshot counts toward neither snapshot limit, while expired Source records still count toward the Source limit, and the fetch attempt limit still bounds how often a Run reads again.

As with an Evidence Revocation (ADR 0046), the Context Journal is not edited. After the basis ends, later model requests carry an expired placeholder, distinct from the revoked one, instead of the research result. An Execution Summary that absorbed an expired result is discarded, and its range is restored from the Context Journal with the placeholder in place of that result. The Run continues without pausing. Answers already published and the model's own Model Turns stay.

The original inline Context Journal exception is superseded by [ADR 0083](0083-research-journals-retain-content-addressed-references-and-purges-remove-the-owned-bytes.md) (#331). Journal and serialized projection entries retain immutable references; account-terms expiry removes their Research-owned exact payloads and all fitted variants without changing journal/range commitments. Replays receive placeholders after purge. Development databases containing the earlier inline format must be recreated.

## Considered Options

- **Fold the account terms into the Retention Rules.** The Retention Rules are Aidash's own periods, set by whoever runs the Deployment and shortened by Tenants. The account terms are a third party's conditions on different data, and #289 asked for a separate basis. Rejected.
- **Govern search results only.** A declared Research provider that reads pages returns their contents under the same agreement. Rejected.
- **Keep the text of Evidence fragments.** Fragments are copies of what the provider returned, and keeping them would keep the service's results past its terms. Rejected. Fragments now keep a digest of their text instead.
- **Apply only the terms in force at retrieval, or only the current terms.** Only the former would ignore an agreement that shortens storage. Only the latter would let a longer agreement extend data retrieved under a shorter one, and data would never expire once its declaration is removed. Rejected in favour of the shorter of the two.
- **Record the termination in the dashboard, with history.** Account terms already live in the server settings (ADR 0045, ADR 0055). Rejected.
- **Purge at once when the termination is recorded.** The terms allow the data until the deadline, and a single deadline formula keeps the sweeper simple. Rejected.
- **Also redact the Context Journal.** This was rejected for the same reasons as in ADR 0046. Holding results by reference is deferred to the follow-up.
- **Keep an Execution Summary that absorbed expired results.** The summary is a compressed copy of those results. Rejected.
- **Stop the Run's research, as a revocation does.** Expiry is routine and the Source can lawfully be read again. Rejected.

## Review follow-up (#391, 2026-10-23)

A recorded account termination permanently retires that account ID. A renewed agreement uses a new ID; a new profile cannot reuse a terminated ID to dispatch requests. Provider retention uses the durable dispatch time conservatively, so settling an abandoned attempt on a later replay never renews the age of artifacts already stored. An expired attempt cannot record late sources or snapshots. A snapshot's first ordinary retention purge reason does not hide later account expiry, which is determined from its originating attempt. Expired page-image citations retain their identity and digest and report `expired`, as passages do.

Completed invocation replay revalidates its result under the same native research lock as settlement, and inference rechecks expiry after context reads and compaction. A later Evidence Revocation also recognizes expired placeholders and removes their original disclosed arguments.
